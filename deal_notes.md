# StreamVault – Licensing Deal Notes

**Prepared for:** Kwame Boateng, VP Content Acquisition  
**Purpose:** Support the $14 million licensing review  
**Evidence base:** StreamVault film analysis of 112 successfully retrieved candidate titles

## 1. Commercial opportunity

The analysis suggests that franchise-associated films deserve priority during licensing discussions.

The three strongest franchises by aggregate historical theatrical revenue-to-budget ratio were:

| Franchise | Films analysed | Aggregate ratio |
|---|---:|---:|
| Avengers | 4 | 7.13x |
| Harry Potter | 8 | 6.06x |
| Pirates of the Caribbean | 5 | 3.62x |

These results provide a starting point for prioritising franchise discussions.

However, historical theatrical performance is not a direct measure of streaming demand, licensing profitability or future audience engagement.

## 2. Recommended negotiation priorities

### Priority 1: Investigate promising franchise bundles

- Prioritise discussions around franchises with strong historical performance.
- Request the complete list of titles included in each proposed bundle.
- Compare bundle pricing with individual-title licensing prices.
- Assess whether the entire bundle supports StreamVault's content strategy.

### Priority 2: Evaluate individual title value

- Do not assume every film in a successful franchise performs equally well.
- Examine individual title performance before accepting bundle terms.
- Consider whether older titles offer sufficient value to justify their licensing costs.
- Request additional audience and streaming performance evidence.

### Priority 3: Investigate titles without recorded collection membership

The analysis identified 16 films without recorded collection membership.

These should not automatically be treated as standalone titles. Their franchise relationships should be verified before making individual licensing decisions.

## 3. Information to request from studios

Before agreeing to any licensing arrangement, request:

| Information | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Licensing price per title | Establishes the actual acquisition cost |
| Total bundle price | Allows comparison with individual-title pricing |
| Available territories | Confirms where StreamVault can distribute the content |
| Licence duration | Establishes how long the rights remain valid |
| Exclusivity conditions | Determines whether competing platforms can offer the same content |
| Streaming performance history | Provides evidence of digital audience demand |
| Rights availability | Confirms whether the studio can legally offer the proposed rights |

## 4. Commercial risks and cautions

The analysis has several limitations that should influence negotiations:

- Historical theatrical revenue does not establish future streaming revenue.
- Production budgets are not licensing costs.
- Financial figures have not been adjusted for inflation.
- Aggregate franchise performance can conceal differences between individual films.
- Missing collection information may affect franchise classification.
- Five candidate movie requests failed during extraction and require further investigation.
- The analysis does not independently verify studio ownership, rights availability or exclusivity.

The statistical outlier analysis also identified seven unusual budget, revenue and runtime values. These were retained because unusual values are not automatically incorrect.

## 5. Questions for the licensing review

Kwame should be prepared to ask:

1. What is the total licensing cost, both individually and as a bundle?
2. Which territories and distribution rights are included?
3. How long will the licensing agreement remain valid?
4. Are the rights exclusive to StreamVault?
5. Can the studio provide historical streaming performance?
6. Are all proposed franchise titles available?
7. Are there restrictions on renewal, sublicensing or early termination?

## 6. Decision guidance

The current analysis supports prioritising certain franchises for further investigation. It does not justify committing the $14 million budget based on historical theatrical performance alone.

Before approving a deal:

- Verify the available rights and proposed licence terms.
- Obtain actual licensing prices.
- Compare bundle and individual-title economics.
- Assess streaming audience demand.
- Evaluate the expected commercial value against the proposed acquisition cost.
- Resolve material gaps in candidate data.

## 7. Final position

Use the film analysis to guide negotiation priorities, not as a substitute for commercial due diligence.

The strongest historical performers deserve further attention, but the final licensing decision should depend on actual rights, acquisition costs, audience demand and expected streaming value.

**Decision principle:** Prioritise opportunities using historical evidence, but commit funding only when the commercial terms support the investment.
